Email to Parents About a Struggling Student

Telling a parent that their child is struggling is hard, and it's easy to sound either too harsh or too vague. Copy the prompt below into any AI assistant (ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini), change the parts in [brackets], and you'll get a calm, supportive first draft in minutes.

The prompt

Act as a compassionate, professional senior classroom teacher. Write a formal yet supportive email to the parent of a [Target Grade Level/Age] student named [Student Name] who is currently struggling with [Specific Issue, e.g., low reading comprehension scores or incomplete homework assignments]. The email must avoid sounding overly punitive. Instead, use a collaborative tone: state the current observation clearly, note that you see the student's potential, and offer 2 specific, actionable ways the parent can support their child's English practice at home. Conclude with an open invitation to schedule a brief phone call or meeting.

Why it works

The prompt asks the AI to do four things in order: state the problem clearly, name the student's potential, give the parent two small things to try at home, and invite a conversation. The parent finishes the email knowing what is happening and what to do next, without feeling blamed.

What to do with the result

Read it aloud once. Replace anything that doesn't sound like you, and add one real example, such as a piece of work or a date, so the email is specific. Then send it from your own school email address.

A mistake to avoid

Sending the draft without adding a real example. "Your child is struggling with reading" is easy to ignore, while "In last week's reading task, he finished only two of five questions" gives the parent something to act on. Also, type [Student Name] as a placeholder and add the real name yourself afterwards, so you don't put a child's details into an AI tool.
